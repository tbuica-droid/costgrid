How it works

Three steps. You do two of them.

CostGrid doesn't replace anything. Your software keeps using the same AI providers, the same accounts and the same keys. It just goes through CostGrid on the way, so every call gets priced and checked against your limits first.

01

Open the app and paste your key

Sign in here, download CostGrid, open it, and paste the key you already use with your AI provider. The app checks the key works and shows what you've spent over the last 90 days, straight from the provider's own records.

You
02

Point your software at CostGrid

One setting in whatever software makes your AI calls: the address it sends them to. The app shows the exact line, ready to hand to the person who looks after that software. From then on every call is priced and logged, and each one can carry a team and a tool name so you see who spent it.

Whoever runs your software · one setting
03

Set the limits

Give each team or tool a monthly cap, and choose what happens at the limit: stop, or switch to a cheaper model so your customers still get an answer. Every change is written to a log that can't be quietly edited.

    Proof

    Measured, not marketed.

    Every number on this page comes from a measurement we can repeat. Where we don't have one, the space stays empty.

    Each AI tool does only what you allowed

    CostGrid records which tool each AI assistant reached for, and refuses anything outside the list you approved, before it costs you a token. Below, three assistants and what each may and may not touch.

    Nobody will notice it's there

    An AI call takes half a second to several seconds. CostGrid adds about a thousandth of a second on top. To make sure that number is CostGrid's own work, the test also runs a do-nothing pass-through and subtracts it. These are the results.

    Delay added by CostGridTypicalWorst 1 in 100
    One call at a time1.2 ms2 ms
    Until the first word of a streamed answer0.8 ms1.6 ms
    Ten calls at once1.3 ms4 ms
    Fifty calls at onceNot published yet, see below

    Why the last row is empty. On one computer, the test itself competes with the thing being tested, and the noise is bigger than the result. We'd rather leave it empty than print a number your own test would contradict.

    When a cap is hit, the call is refused before it reaches the provider, and the attempt is recorded either way. That's the default, not an option you have to find.

    Pricing

    Flat rate. 0% of your AI bill.

    Cost tools that take a percentage of spend get paid more when you spend more. Ours doesn't. The price is the price, whether you burn $2k or $200k a month.

    Most Macs made since 2020 have an Apple chip. Not sure? Click the Apple menu, then About This Mac: it says Apple or Intel next to "Chip" or "Processor".

    Download for Apple chip Download for Intel
    The first time you open it

    CostGrid isn't registered with Apple yet, so macOS will say it can't check the app and stop it from opening. That's expected. To get past it once: open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the message about CostGrid, and click Open Anyway. After that it opens like any other app.

    Windows is next. Nothing else to install: the app holds everything, and your provider key is encrypted with a key kept in your Mac's keychain.

    Where this came from

    A cost model first, a product second.

    CostGrid began as research into why AI bills rise while token prices fall, across six modules covering price decay, routing, volume, spend, seat pricing and EBITDA impact, driven from a single assumptions sheet. The routing maths in the product is that model. The difference is that the product measures your real numbers instead of assuming them.
