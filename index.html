How it saves money

Six ways to a smaller AI bill.

Point your agents at CostGrid (a one-line change for whoever set them up) and it stops just showing the waste and starts cutting it. Nothing changes until you say so.

Half price

Work that can wait

Overnight reports, bulk summaries, tagging. CostGrid sends them through OpenAI's and Claude's batch services, which charge half the normal price.

A tenth

Instructions you send every time

Most agents send the same long instructions with every request. CostGrid marks them so Claude charges a tenth of the price for the repeats.

Less thinking

Agents that overthink easy jobs

You pay for every word an AI thinks. When an agent thinks at length to give a short answer, CostGrid turns it down a notch.

Cheaper model

Simple work on an expensive model

CostGrid tries a cheaper model on a small share of the work, side by side, and only switches when the answers are just as good.

No runaways

Agents stuck in a loop

Set a limit per job, per day or per month, and a runaway agent stops before it runs up the bill.

Less waste

Money spent on jobs that fail

See how much of your bill goes on attempts that never finish, agent by agent, and fix the worst one first.

How it works

Up and running in three steps.

Step 1

Connect OpenAI or Claude

Add a key, or drop in the usage file you can download from either one. CostGrid brings in your last 90 days and shows what each of your agents costs. Your key stays in your Mac's Keychain.

Step 2

See what each finished job costs

Point your agents at CostGrid, a one-line change for whoever set them up. It sees which jobs got done and which failed, and adds failed attempts to the cost of the ones that worked.

Step 3

Switch on the savings

CostGrid suggests ways to save. Each one runs as a test first, so you see the saving before anything changes, and it keeps checking after you switch it on.

Privacy and keys

Your data stays yours.

Your prompts only go where you choose

When one of your agents asks AI to do something, CostGrid passes the request to the AI company that agent already uses, OpenAI or Anthropic. It only sends it somewhere else if you've said that's fine for that agent and set up a rule to do it, for example to the other of those two, or to a service running open models that you've connected with your own account. That service then looks after the request under its own terms. Your keys only ever go to the service they belong to. We never see them.

We don't keep your prompts or answers

CostGrid reads each request and answer as it goes by, to work out what it cost and whether it came back right. Then it lets them go. If you turn on grading, a stronger AI you already use compares two answers on a small sample of test jobs, and all we keep is which one it preferred. If you also turn on spot checks, those two answers wait in the app's memory, never saved to disk, so you can read them yourself. We hold at most 50, for no more than a day, and they're gone as soon as the app restarts or you switch spot checks off.

It runs on your Mac

CostGrid works out your costs on your own computer, not on our servers.

Repeat jobs, spotted without the words

To notice when the same job is asked twice, CostGrid keeps a one-way code of the job's first message. It can't be turned back into words, it never leaves your Mac, and you can switch it off.

Keys locked in your Mac's Keychain

Your AI keys are stored encrypted, and the key that unlocks them sits in your Mac's Keychain, next to your saved passwords. A copied CostGrid folder is useless on another Mac.

One key per agent, with an end date

We suggest it. You see each agent's cost on its own, you can switch one off without touching the rest, and a key with an end date stops working by itself.

Questions

Straight answers.

How does it lower my bill?

Six ways: half price for work that can wait, a tenth of the price for instructions sent again and again, less thinking for agents that overthink, cheaper models where they do just as well, limits that stop runaway agents, and a clear view of the money spent on jobs that fail. All but the last need your agents to go through CostGrid.

Will it make my AI worse?

It's built not to. Every saving starts as a test that changes nothing, so you see what it would do. A cheaper model is tried on a small share of the work first, side by side, and only used if the answers are just as good. If anything gets worse after a change, CostGrid switches it back on its own and tells you.

Does it read my data?

It looks at each request and answer as it passes, to see what it cost and whether it worked, and doesn't keep either. Your requests go to the AI company your agent already uses, and only go anywhere else if you've said so for that agent. If you turn on spot checks, a few answers are held in memory, never saved, for up to a day so you can read them yourself.

Do I need to be technical?

No. Download the app and connect OpenAI or Claude. If a freelancer or someone else set up your AI, CostGrid writes a message you can send them, saying exactly what it needs.

What does it work with?

OpenAI and Claude, including agents built in tools like n8n, as long as they use your own account. To read your usage directly, CostGrid needs a key made by an admin or owner of that account. Without one, drop in the usage file anyone with billing access can download.

Does it stop made-up answers?

Not directly. CostGrid doesn't check whether an answer is true. It does check whether an answer came back cut off, refused or in the wrong format, counts what failed attempts cost you, and shows which agents waste the most.

Is there a Windows version?

It's on the way. CostGrid is Mac only for now.

Is it open source?

You can read all of the code. It's published under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1-ALv2), and each version becomes Apache 2.0 open source two years after it's released. The method behind the numbers is free for anyone to use, with credit.
