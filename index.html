The problem

Per-token prices keep falling and AI bills keep rising. Unit price is the wrong thing to watch: spend is price multiplied by volume, and volume wins. An agent that retries, a prompt that grew, a feature shipped on the frontier model "just to be safe" — none of it shows up until the invoice does.

By then the provider bill is a single number for the whole company. It cannot tell you which team spent it, which feature, or which of them was worth it.

The usual answers do not work. A dashboard built on the provider's billing export arrives days late and has no idea what a "team" is. Spreadsheets estimate tokens instead of counting them. Neither can refuse a call, which means neither can stop anything.

Reporting a saving is advice. CostGrid is in the request path, so it can actually make one.

How it works

1

Change one line

Point your client's base URL at CostGrid. It forwards to the same provider with the same request, streaming included.

client = Anthropic(
  base_url="https://costgrid.acme.dev",
  api_key="unused",
)

Your provider key lives in CostGrid, so the services calling through it never hold one. Anthropic and OpenAI today.

2

Label the traffic

Two optional headers turn one provider invoice into per-team, per-feature chargeback.

x-costgrid-agent: chat-bot
x-costgrid-department: Support

Unlabelled calls are still metered — they show up as unattributed, a visible cost line rather than a silent gap.

3

Set the rules

Budgets per team or per agent, model allowlists, output caps. Start in monitor to watch, then enforce.

costgrid policy budget \
  dept:Engineering 500.00 \
  --fallback claude-haiku-4-5

A blocked call is refused before it is forwarded, so it costs nothing.

What you get

Exact metering, not estimates

Token counts come from each provider's own usage object. Five buckets are priced independently — input, output, 5-minute and 1-hour cache writes, cache reads — because they cost different amounts and a cache-heavy workload is mispriced by anything that lumps them together. Money is integer arithmetic end to end; nothing rounds until it is displayed.

Budgets that do not break production

The reason nobody turns on a hard cap is that it breaks the product at 3am. A CostGrid cap can downgrade instead: over-budget traffic is served by a cheaper model and keeps answering, with a response header saying so. It refuses to make a substitution it cannot make safely — across providers, or to a model it cannot price.

Savings you can audit

A routing rule sends matching traffic to a cheaper model and records the counterfactual: what those exact tokens would have cost on the model the caller asked for. Always dry-run first — monitor changes nothing and still measures what it would have saved.

A statement finance can reconcile

A closed calendar month in UTC, not a trailing window, because that is how the provider invoices. Spend by team with movement against last month, budget status, what routing saved — as text, CSV or JSON.

The questions you are about to ask

Does our prompt and response data go to you?
No. You run CostGrid inside your own infrastructure; there is no CostGrid service in the middle. Request and response bodies are forwarded to the provider and streamed back untouched — what is stored is token counts, model names, cost, and the labels you attach. Not prompts, not completions.
What does it do to latency?
One local network hop. Streaming bytes are passed through as they arrive rather than buffered, so time-to-first-token is essentially unchanged.
What happens if CostGrid goes down?
It is in the request path, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest: if it is down, those calls fail. That is the price of being able to refuse a call rather than just report it afterwards. Run it beside the service that calls it, and start every rule in monitor until you trust it.
Are the prices right?
53 models across Anthropic and OpenAI, checked against the providers' published tables by a script that fails on a discrepancy or a stale verification date. The dashboard says so on the page when the catalog is more than 45 days old, because a stale catalog does not fail loudly — it just bills last quarter's rates.
What is it bad at?
Bedrock and Vertex are not supported yet, only direct Anthropic and OpenAI. It governs API spend, not seat licences — if your AI bill is people using a chat product in a browser, there is nothing here to meter. And it is one node on SQLite today: fine for a team, not yet a high-availability deployment.

Where this came from

CostGrid started as research into why AI bills rise while token prices fall: a cost model covering price decay, routing economics and volume growth to 2030. The routing maths in the product is that model, and the dashboard's Routing tab measures your real substitution share against the optimum it computes.

Read the thesis — why unit price is the wrong thing to watch — or the research dashboard it came with. Both use illustrative figures: they argue a case rather than report a deployment. Every figure in the product is measured from your own traffic, and an empty database shows an empty state rather than a plausible chart.
