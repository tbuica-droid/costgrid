01 · How it works

Three small changes, none of them to your own code.

Your calls still go to the same place and come back the same way. CostGrid sits in the middle and reads what each one used.

01  Point the base URL

client = Anthropic(
  base_url="https://costgrid.acme.dev",
  api_key="unused",
)

Your AI account key lives inside CostGrid, so the services calling through it never hold one. Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS Bedrock and Google Vertex.

02  Label the traffic

x-costgrid-agent: chat-bot
x-costgrid-department: Support

Two optional headers turn one AI bill into a bill you can split by team. Calls with no label are still measured, just marked unassigned, so nothing goes missing.

03  Set the rules

costgrid policy budget \
  dept:Engineering 500.00 \
  --fallback claude-haiku-4-5

Budgets per team or agent, model allowlists, output caps. Start every rule in monitor to watch what it would have done.

04 · What you actually run

Nobody can tell you what their AI can reach.

Most companies now run AI that acts on its own: it calls other software, looks things up, and hands work to other AI. Those are agents, and almost nobody has an accurate picture of what theirs can touch. They have a diagram from six months ago and a lot of hope. CostGrid draws the real one from your own traffic: which agents use which AI, what tools they can reach, and who hands work to whom. Not from your documentation, but from what your software actually did this week.

Then you can draw a line around any of it. One command stops an agent being given a tool, and stops every agent it passes work to from being given it either. It works because a model cannot use a tool it was never handed.

costgrid policy deny-tool agent:support refund_customer
Tool names only. What they were called with is never stored.

05 · Straight answers

The questions you are about to ask.

Do you see our data?

No. CostGrid runs on your own servers. There is no CostGrid service in the middle. It records how many tokens were used, which model, what it cost and your labels. Never the prompts or the answers.

Will it slow us down?

One hop on your own network, and streamed responses pass straight through. In practice, no.

What if it goes down?

Those calls fail, and we will not pretend otherwise. That is the price of being able to stop a call instead of reporting on it afterwards. Run it next to the service that uses it, and leave every rule in watch-only mode until you trust it.

Are the prices right?

53 models, checked against the providers’ published rates by a script that fails the build if anything drifts. If the price list goes more than 45 days without a check, the dashboard says so on the page.

What is it not good at?

It watches what your software spends on AI, not what your staff spend on chat subscriptions. If your bill is people typing into a browser, there is nothing here to measure. AWS Bedrock and Google Vertex work but have not yet been run against a live account, so there is a one-minute check that tells you if yours is fine. And it runs on a single server today: right for one team, not yet right if losing that server would be a crisis.
